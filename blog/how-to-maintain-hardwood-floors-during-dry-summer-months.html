How To Maintain Hardwood Floors During Dry Summer Months

Jul 12, 2026 · All Floors 4 Less

July in Woodland Hills brings long sunny days, warm evenings, and the kind of dry summer heat that makes our area feel unmistakably like the San Fernando Valley. While many homeowners focus on keeping the house cool, your hardwood floors also need attention during this season. Dry air, direct sunlight, heavy foot traffic, and air conditioning can all affect the beauty and stability of hardwood flooring.

At All Floors 4 Less in Woodland Hills, CA, we help homeowners choose, install, and care for flooring that fits Southern California living. If you already have hardwood floors—or you are thinking about upgrading your home with hardwood—summer maintenance is one of the most important ways to protect your investment.

Why Dry Summer Weather Affects Hardwood Floors

Hardwood is a natural material. Even after it is cut, milled, finished, and installed, it continues to respond to changes in temperature and humidity. During July in Woodland Hills, humidity levels can drop, especially when the air conditioner is running throughout the day. When the air becomes too dry, hardwood flooring may lose moisture and contract.

That contraction can lead to:

  • Small gaps between boards, especially in solid hardwood flooring.
  • Minor cupping or shrinking if indoor conditions fluctuate too much.
  • Surface dullness from dust, sunlight, and dry indoor air.
  • Increased risk of scratches when dirt and grit are tracked inside.

The good news is that with the right routine, most seasonal issues can be minimized. Hardwood floors are durable and long-lasting, but they perform best when your home environment stays reasonably consistent.

Key Takeaways:

  • Keep indoor humidity as consistent as possible during hot, dry July weather in Woodland Hills.
  • Protect hardwood floors from direct sunlight with window coverings, rugs, or UV-protective treatments.
  • Clean regularly with hardwood-safe products and avoid excess water or harsh cleaners.
  • Use mats, felt pads, and area rugs to reduce scratches from summer foot traffic.

Keep Indoor Humidity Balanced

One of the best things you can do for hardwood floors during a dry summer is to monitor indoor humidity. Most hardwood floors are happiest when indoor humidity stays in a moderate range, often around 35% to 55%, depending on the product and manufacturer guidelines. Woodland Hills summers can push indoor conditions below that range, especially when the AC runs constantly.

A simple indoor hygrometer can help you track your home’s humidity level. If the air becomes too dry, consider using a humidifier in key areas of the home, particularly rooms with large hardwood surfaces. You do not need to make the air feel damp—just balanced.

Also, avoid extreme temperature swings. If you leave the house for the day, try not to shut the AC off completely if indoor heat will climb dramatically. Consistency is better for both your comfort and your flooring.

Protect Hardwood Floors from Strong July Sunlight

Woodland Hills gets plenty of summer sunshine, and while natural light makes a room feel open and beautiful, direct UV exposure can fade or discolor hardwood over time. Some species darken, while others lighten. Furniture and rugs can also create uneven color patterns if the same areas are exposed every day.

To reduce sun damage:

  • Close blinds, shutters, or curtains during peak afternoon sun.
  • Use UV-filtering window film on large windows or sliding glass doors.
  • Rotate area rugs and furniture occasionally to allow more even aging.
  • Consider lighter window treatments that diffuse sunlight without darkening the room completely.

Protecting your floors from sunlight is especially important in south- and west-facing rooms, which tend to get the strongest afternoon exposure in our area.

Clean Smart: Less Water, More Consistency

Summer often means more activity at home—kids out of school, pets going in and out, backyard gatherings, and guests walking through with dust from driveways and patios. That extra traffic can bring grit onto hardwood floors. Grit acts like sandpaper under shoes and can dull the finish.

Regular cleaning is simple but important. Sweep or vacuum with a hardwood-safe attachment several times a week. For deeper cleaning, use a product specifically designed for hardwood floors. Avoid soaking the floor or using steam mops, since excess moisture can damage wood and finish.

Here are a few summer cleaning habits we recommend:

  • Place mats at entry doors, especially near patios, garages, and front entrances.
  • Ask family members to remove shoes when possible.
  • Wipe spills immediately with a soft, dry or slightly damp cloth.
  • Use microfiber dust mops to collect fine dust without scratching the finish.
  • Avoid vinegar, ammonia, abrasive cleaners, and oil soaps unless approved by your floor manufacturer.

Use Rugs and Furniture Pads to Prevent Scratches

During July, many homes see more movement between indoor and outdoor spaces. Pool days, barbecues, family visits, and summer get-togethers can all increase wear on hardwood floors. High-traffic areas such as hallways, kitchens, living rooms, and entryways benefit from extra protection.

Area rugs are an excellent option, but make sure they have hardwood-safe rug pads underneath. Some rubber or low-quality pads can discolor finishes, so choose breathable pads made for wood floors. Felt pads under chairs, tables, and sofas also help prevent scratches when furniture shifts.

If you have pets, keep nails trimmed. Dogs running across hardwood can create surface marks, especially if dust or grit is already on the floor. A consistent cleaning routine plus protective pads can make a big difference.

Watch for Signs Your Hardwood Needs Professional Attention

Not every change in hardwood flooring is a reason to panic. Some seasonal movement is normal. However, if you notice larger gaps, boards lifting, deep scratches, fading, or finish wear, it may be time for a professional opinion.

Signs to look for include:

  • Gaps that do not close when humidity improves.
  • Boards that feel loose, raised, or uneven.
  • Discoloration in sunny areas or under rugs.
  • Cloudy finish, peeling, or worn-through protective coating.
  • Persistent squeaks or movement underfoot.

Thinking About New Hardwood Floors? Choose the Right Product for Woodland Hills

If you are considering new hardwood floors, July is a great time to think about how flooring will perform in our local climate. Not all hardwood products are the same. Solid hardwood, engineered hardwood, laminate, and luxury vinyl plank each respond differently to heat, dryness, moisture, and traffic.

For many Woodland Hills homes, engineered hardwood can be a smart choice because it offers the beauty of real wood with added dimensional stability. It is often better suited for environments where temperature and humidity fluctuate. That said, the right choice depends on your home, lifestyle, budget, subfloor, and design goals.
