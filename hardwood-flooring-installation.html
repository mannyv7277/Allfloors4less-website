Hardwood buying guide

Choosing the right hardwood for your home

Hardwood is a long-term investment, so it pays to choose carefully. Here's what we walk every customer through at the showroom before a single board goes down.

Solid vs. engineered hardwood

Solid hardwood is one piece of wood from top to bottom and can be sanded and refinished many times over its life. Engineered hardwood has a real-wood top layer bonded to a stable core, which makes it less likely to expand and shrink with temperature and humidity changes. Engineered is often the better pick over concrete slabs — common in Southern California homes — and in rooms that see more moisture, while solid shines in living spaces over a wood subfloor.

Species, plank width & finish

Oak is the most popular choice for its strength and visible grain, maple has a smoother, more even look, and cherry brings a warm tone that deepens over time. Wider planks give a modern, open feel, while narrower boards feel more traditional. Finish matters too: matte and satin sheens hide everyday scratches and dust far better than high gloss.

What affects the price

Your total depends on the square footage, the hardwood you choose, the condition of the subfloor, whether old flooring needs to be removed, and details like stairs, trim and transitions between rooms. Hardwood know-how

Getting the most from your hardwood floors

A great hardwood floor depends on more than the boards. These are the details that decide how your floor looks and performs years from now.

Why the subfloor matters

Hardwood is only as good as what's underneath it. An uneven or flexing subfloor leads to squeaks, gaps and boards that move under your feet. Before installation, the subfloor should be checked for flatness, moisture and loose spots, and any problem areas fixed. Over concrete, a moisture barrier or underlayment protects the wood from moisture rising out of the slab, which is one of the most common causes of cupping in Southern California homes.

Acclimation and seasonal movement

Wood is a natural material that expands in humid weather and shrinks in dry weather. Letting the flooring adjust to your home's normal temperature and humidity before it goes down, and leaving proper expansion space at the walls, keeps the floor from buckling in summer or opening wide gaps in winter. Small seasonal gaps between boards are normal, especially during dry Santa Ana conditions.

Nail-down, glue-down or floating

Solid hardwood is usually nailed to a wood subfloor. Engineered hardwood can be glued directly to a concrete slab or installed as a floating floor over an underlayment. Each method has trade-offs in feel, sound, cost and how the floor handles moisture, and the right one depends on your subfloor and the product you choose. We'll recommend the method that fits your home.

Frequently asked questions

Hardwood flooring FAQs

What types of hardwood flooring do you install?

We install a wide variety of hardwood, including oak, maple and cherry, in both solid and engineered options. We'll help you choose the species, plank width and finish that fit your style and your home.

How long does a typical installation take?

Most standard hardwood installations take about 2 to 5 days, depending on the size of the space and the complexity of the job. We'll give you a timeline before we start.

Do I need to do anything to prepare?

Clear the rooms of furniture and small items before installation day. We take care of the subfloor — making sure it's clean, level and ready for your new floor.

How do I maintain my new hardwood floors?

Sweep or vacuum regularly, wipe up spills quickly with a damp (not wet) cloth, and use felt pads under furniture. We'll walk you through care tips after installation.

Can hardwood be installed over a concrete slab?

Yes — engineered hardwood is designed for it. Many Southern California homes are built on concrete slabs, and engineered hardwood is the go-to choice there because it handles temperature and moisture changes better than solid wood.

Should I choose hardwood or laminate?

Hardwood is real wood that can be refinished for decades and adds resale value. Laminate gives a similar look for less money and resists scratches well, which makes it popular with busy families and pets. Visit the showroom and we'll help you compare them side by side.

Is my installation covered by a warranty?

Yes. Every installation comes with our two-year installation warranty, plus the manufacturer's warranty on your flooring materials.

How long does hardwood flooring last?

With normal care, a solid hardwood floor can last for generations, because it can be sanded and refinished many times. Engineered hardwood lasts for decades too; how many times it can be refinished depends on the thickness of its top wood layer.

Can hardwood go in a kitchen?

Yes. Many homeowners love hardwood in an open kitchen. Wipe up spills right away, use rugs at the sink and dishwasher, and choose a durable finish. If you want extra peace of mind, engineered hardwood or a water-resistant laminate are good alternatives.

Does hardwood flooring add value to a home?

Hardwood is one of the most requested features among home buyers, and it can make a home more appealing when you sell. It also holds up well over time and can be refinished instead of replaced.
