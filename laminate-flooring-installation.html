Thickness & wear layer

Thicker laminate planks feel more solid underfoot and sound less hollow, while the clear wear layer on top is what protects against scratches and fading. Higher-traffic rooms and homes with pets benefit from a tougher wear layer.

Water-resistant options

Standard laminate doesn't like standing water, but many newer products are built with sealed edges and water-resistant cores that handle kitchens, laundry rooms and entryways much better. We'll point you to the right ones for wet areas.

What affects the price

Your total depends on the square footage, the laminate line you choose, underlayment, removing the old floor, subfloor leveling, stairs and trim. Our Instant Quote Calculator gives you a quick ballpark, and we confirm the final price after measuring.

Laminate know-how

What to know before you install laminate

Today's laminate looks more like real wood and stone than ever, and it's tougher than many people expect. A few choices make the difference between a floor that lasts and one that doesn't.

How laminate is made

Laminate is built in layers: a stable fiberboard core, a high-resolution photo layer that gives it the look of wood or stone, and a clear wear layer on top that resists scratches, dents and fading. Many lines also have textured surfaces that follow the wood grain, so the floor feels as realistic as it looks. Because the core is engineered, laminate stays flat and stable across large rooms.

The AC rating

Laminate is graded for durability with an AC (abrasion class) rating. AC3 is a solid choice for most homes, including busy living areas and hallways, while AC4 and AC5 handle heavier traffic and light commercial use. If you have large dogs, young kids or a lot of foot traffic, a higher AC rating and a thicker board are worth considering. We'll help you match the rating to your home.

Underlayment and sound

Laminate is installed as a floating floor, so the underlayment beneath it matters. A quality underlayment smooths out minor subfloor imperfections, cuts down on the hollow sound some laminate floors make, and adds a moisture barrier over concrete. Some boards come with underlayment already attached. In upstairs rooms and condos, a sound-rated underlayment helps keep footsteps quiet for the people below.

Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about laminate flooring installation

How long does laminate installation take?

Many laminate jobs take one to three days depending on the size of the space, how much prep the subfloor needs and whether old flooring is coming out. We'll give you a timeline up front.

Is laminate waterproof?

Some laminate is water-resistant and some is not. Water-resistant lines are built for kitchens and entries, but no laminate should sit in standing water. We'll recommend the right product for each room.

Can laminate go over my existing floor?

Often it can go over a flat, solid floor such as vinyl or tile, which can save time and cost. Carpet always needs to come out first. We'll check your floor and let you know.

Is laminate good for pets?

Yes — a quality laminate with a strong wear layer resists scratches from claws better than many other floors, and it's easy to clean.

How do I care for laminate floors?

Sweep or vacuum regularly and clean with a damp — not wet — mop. Wipe spills quickly and use felt pads under furniture.

Is my installation covered by a warranty?

Yes. Every installation comes with our two-year installation warranty, plus the manufacturer's warranty on your flooring materials.

How long does laminate flooring last?

A quality laminate floor typically lasts 15 to 25 years or more, depending on the product, the wear layer and how much traffic it gets. Higher AC ratings hold up longer in busy areas.

Can laminate be installed on stairs?

Yes. Laminate can be installed on stairs with matching stair nose trim, which gives a clean, finished edge. Stairs take more time and detail than flat floors, so they're priced separately.

Is laminate a good choice for rentals?

Yes. Laminate's scratch resistance, easy cleaning and lower cost make it a popular choice for rental properties, and damaged boards can often be replaced individually.
