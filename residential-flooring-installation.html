Kitchens, baths & laundry

These rooms see water and spills, so porcelain tile is a top choice. Water-resistant laminate can work well in kitchens too.

Living areas & hallways

High-traffic spaces call for durable floors. Hardwood adds warmth and value, laminate resists scratches, and continuous flooring through connected rooms makes a home feel bigger.

Bedrooms & what affects the price

Carpet adds softness and quiet in bedrooms, while wood-look floors are easy to keep clean. Your total depends on square footage, materials, removal, subfloor prep, stairs and trim — our Instant Quote Calculator gives a quick ballpark.

Planning your project

Planning new floors for your home

Whether you're updating one room or the whole house, a little planning up front makes the project smoother and the result better. Here's what to think about before installation day.

One floor throughout, or different floors by room

Running the same floor through the main living areas makes a home feel larger and more connected, and it avoids extra transitions between rooms. Some homeowners prefer a mix, such as wood or laminate in living areas, tile in wet areas and carpet in bedrooms. Either can look great; what matters is that the colors and heights work together and the transitions are planned.

Floor height and transitions

Every flooring type has a different thickness. When rooms with different floors meet, the heights need to line up, or be bridged with a transition strip, so there's no tripping edge. New floors can also affect door clearances, baseboards and appliances like dishwashers. Thinking about these details before materials are ordered avoids surprises and keeps the finished floor looking clean.

Living at home during the project

Most flooring projects can be done while you live in the home, especially if the work is done in stages. Plan to clear furniture and belongings from the rooms being worked on, and keep pets and kids away from the work area. For larger projects, ask how the work will be sequenced so you always have access to a kitchen, a bathroom and a place to sleep.

Frequently asked questions

Your flooring questions answered

Can you install flooring in my whole house?

Yes. We handle single rooms and whole-home projects, including running one continuous floor through connected spaces.

How long will my project take?

It depends on the size of the home and the flooring type. Many single-room projects take a day or two; whole-home projects take longer. We'll give you a timeline up front.

Do I need to move my furniture?

Clearing small items, breakables and electronics before we arrive helps the job go faster. Ask us about furniture when you get your estimate.

Which floor is best for kids and pets?

Scratch-resistant laminate and durable tile are popular with families and pets. We'll show you the options at the showroom.

Can you match flooring between rooms?

We'll help you choose floors and transitions that flow well from room to room, even when different rooms need different materials.

Is my installation covered by a warranty?

Yes. Every installation comes with our two-year installation warranty, plus the manufacturer's warranty on your flooring materials.

What's the most durable flooring for a busy home?

Porcelain tile and high-quality laminate are among the most durable choices for busy homes with kids and pets. Engineered hardwood with a tough finish is also a good option if you want real wood.

Should I install flooring before or after painting?

Painting first is usually best, so drips and spills land on the old floor. Baseboards and touch-ups can be done after the new floor is installed.

Can you help me choose colors and styles?

Yes. Visit our Woodland Hills showroom to see and feel the options in person, and we'll help you choose floors that fit your style, your lifestyle and your budget.
