Tile buying guide

Choosing the right tile for your space

Tile comes in endless styles. These are the choices that matter most.

Ceramic vs. porcelain vs. stone

Ceramic is affordable and great for walls and lighter-traffic floors. Porcelain is denser and absorbs less water, which makes it a strong pick for busy floors and wet rooms. Natural stone like marble or travertine brings one-of-a-kind character but needs sealing and a little more care.

Size, layout & grout

Large-format tiles mean fewer grout lines and a more open, modern look, but they need a very flat floor. Patterns like herringbone or offset layouts change the whole feel of a room. Grout color can blend in or make the pattern stand out.

What affects the price

Your total depends on the square footage, the tile you choose, the layout, floor prep and leveling, removing old flooring, and details like trim and thresholds. Our Instant Quote Calculator gives you a quick ballpark, and we confirm the final price after measuring.

Tile know-how

What goes into a tile floor that lasts

Tile can last for decades, but only if it's installed on the right foundation with the right materials. Here's what separates a long-lasting tile floor from one that cracks.

Preparing the surface

Tile needs a flat, solid base. Any flex or unevenness in the subfloor can crack tile or grout over time. Depending on the room, preparation may include leveling low spots, adding a cement backer board over wood subfloors, or using an uncoupling membrane that helps protect the tile from small movements and cracks in a concrete slab. Good prep is the part you never see, and the part that matters most.

Choosing the right grout

Standard cement grout is affordable and comes in many colors, but it's porous and should be sealed. Epoxy and high-performance grouts resist stains and moisture much better, which makes them a great choice for kitchens, bathrooms and entryways. Grout color changes the whole look: a matching grout blends tiles together for a seamless feel, while a contrasting grout highlights the pattern.

Slip resistance and finish

Polished tile looks sleek but can be slippery when wet. For bathrooms, laundry rooms, entries and patios, a matte or textured finish gives better grip. Tile manufacturers rate slip resistance, so it's easy to compare options. Smaller tiles with more grout lines also add traction in showers and wet areas. We'll point you to tiles that suit how each room is actually used.

Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about tile flooring installation

How long does tile installation take?

Most tile floors take a few days, because the setting material and grout need time to cure between steps. Larger or more detailed layouts take longer. We'll give you a timeline before we start.

Which tile is best for bathrooms and kitchens?

Porcelain is a favorite for wet and busy rooms because it absorbs very little water. For bathrooms we'll steer you toward tile with good slip resistance.

Can you install wood-look tile?

Yes. Wood-look porcelain gives you the style of hardwood with the water resistance of tile — a popular choice for kitchens, baths and whole-home flooring.

Do I need to seal my tile?

Most porcelain and ceramic tile doesn't need sealing, but grout and natural stone usually do. We'll let you know what your floor needs.

How do I keep tile and grout clean?

Sweep regularly and mop with a pH-neutral cleaner. Avoid harsh acids on natural stone, and wipe spills on grout quickly.

Is my installation covered by a warranty?

Yes. Every installation comes with our two-year installation warranty, plus the manufacturer's warranty on your flooring materials.

Can tile be installed over existing tile?

Sometimes. If the old tile is flat, solid and well bonded, new tile can go over it, but it raises the floor height and can affect doors and transitions. In many cases removing the old tile gives a better long-term result.

Is tile good for homes with pets?

Yes. Porcelain and ceramic tile are scratch resistant, easy to clean and don't hold pet hair or odors, which makes tile one of the most pet-friendly floors you can choose.

Does tile feel cold underfoot?

Tile stays cooler than wood or carpet, which many Southern California homeowners appreciate in summer. Area rugs add warmth in winter, and radiant floor heating can be installed under tile if you want a warm floor year-round.
